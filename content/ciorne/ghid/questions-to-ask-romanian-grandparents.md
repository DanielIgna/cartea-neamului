If you grew up in the United States or Canada, your Romanian may be good enough for Sunday lunch but not for a long conversation about your grandmother's childhood. Your grandparents, on the other hand, tell their best stories in Romanian, with the words of their village. This guide gives you forty questions in both languages, so you can ask in Romanian and let them answer the way they speak.

The questions follow a life from beginning to end: the family and the village, school and work, the hard years, leaving home and the new country. You do not need all forty. Pick five for each visit or call and let the stories wander.

## Before you start

- **Record, do not only take notes.** A voice carries what writing loses: the accent, the laugh, the pause before a hard memory. A phone is enough. See [recording your grandparents' voices](/ghid/cum-inregistrezi-glasul-bunicilor).
- **One story at a time.** Short recordings of two to five minutes are easier to listen to and to find later than a two-hour interview.
- **Ask for names, places and years.** "What was your grandfather called?" leads further than "Tell me about the old days."
- **Let them choose the language.** Many grandparents switch to Romanian when the story matters. Let them.

## The family and the village

1. What were your parents' names, and where were they born? — *Cum îi chema pe părinții tăi și unde s-au născut?*
2. What were your grandparents' names? Did you know them? — *Cum îi chema pe bunicii tăi? I-ai cunoscut?*
3. What was your mother's maiden name? — *Ce nume avea mama ta înainte să se mărite?*
4. What was the name of your village, and which county was it in? — *Cum se numea satul tău și în ce județ era?*
5. Did your family have a nickname in the village? — *Cum le spunea lumea alor tăi în sat? Aveați poreclă?*
6. What did the house you grew up in look like? — *Cum arăta casa în care ai crescut?*
7. How many brothers and sisters did you have? Where did they end up? — *Câți frați și surori ai avut? Unde au ajuns?*
8. Who were your godparents? — *Cine ți-au fost nașii?*

Nicknames and maiden names matter more than they seem: in the village registers a family is often easier to find under the nickname everybody used. See [nicknames and names in the village](/ghid/porecle-si-nume-in-sat) and [finding your grandmother's maiden name](/ghid/cum-gasesti-numele-de-fata-al-bunicii).

## Childhood and school

9. What is your earliest memory? — *Care e cea mai veche amintire a ta?*
10. What games did you play as a child? — *Ce jocuri jucați când erai mic?*
11. What was school like? Who was your favourite teacher? — *Cum era la școală? Ce învățător îți plăcea cel mai mult?*
12. What did you eat on an ordinary day, and what on holidays? — *Ce mâncați într-o zi obișnuită și ce de sărbători?*
13. How did your family celebrate Christmas and Easter? — *Cum se țineau Crăciunul și Paștele la voi acasă?*
14. What work did you do around the house as a child? — *Ce treabă făceai pe lângă casă când erai copil?*

## Work, love and marriage

15. What was your first job? — *Care a fost prima ta slujbă?*
16. How did you meet grandpa or grandma? — *Cum v-ați cunoscut tu și bunicul (bunica)?*
17. What was your wedding like? Where did it take place? — *Cum a fost nunta voastră? Unde s-a ținut?*
18. Where did you live after you married? — *Unde ați locuit după ce v-ați căsătorit?*
19. What did your parents do for a living? — *Cu ce se ocupau părinții tăi?*
20. Was there a skill in the family passed from parent to child? — *Era vreo meserie în familie, dată din tată în fiu?*

## The hard years

These questions touch painful memories. Ask gently, and accept a "not today".

21. What do you remember about the war, or what did your parents tell you? — *Ce îți amintești din război sau ce ți-au povestit ai tăi?*
22. Did anyone in the family serve in the army? Where? — *A fost cineva din familie în armată? Unde?*
23. What happened to your family's land during collectivization? — *Ce s-a întâmplat cu pământul familiei la colectivizare?*
24. What was daily life like under communism? — *Cum era viața de zi cu zi pe vremea comunismului?*
25. What did you have to queue for? — *La ce stăteați la coadă?*
26. Where were you in December 1989? — *Unde erai în decembrie 1989?*

Collectivization and the war left traces in the archives: agricultural registers, military records, deportation lists. See [collectivization and the agricultural registers](/ghid/colectivizarea-si-registrele-agricole) and [military records](/ghid/registrele-militare).

## Leaving home

27. When did you decide to leave Romania, and why? — *Când te-ai hotărât să pleci din România și de ce?*
28. Who left first, and who stayed behind? — *Cine a plecat primul și cine a rămas acasă?*
29. What did you take with you? — *Ce ai luat cu tine?*
30. How did you travel, and where did you arrive first? — *Cum ați călătorit și unde ați ajuns prima dată?*
31. What surprised you most in the new country? — *Ce te-a mirat cel mai tare în țara nouă?*
32. What was your first home and first job here? — *Care a fost prima ta casă și prima slujbă aici?*
33. Did you change your name or its spelling here? — *Ți-ai schimbat numele sau felul în care se scrie?*

Spelling changes are why many families cannot find their ancestors in records. Our guide on [Romanian surnames in America](/ghid/romanian-surnames-changed-in-america) shows how to search under every version.

## Looking back

34. What do you miss most from home? — *Ce îți lipsește cel mai mult de acasă?*
35. Which Romanian word or saying do you want us to keep? — *Ce vorbă sau ce zicală românească vrei să păstrăm?*
36. Which recipe should never be forgotten in our family? — *Ce rețetă nu trebuie uitată niciodată în familia noastră?*
37. Is there a song you sang as a child? Can you sing it? — *Ce cântec cântai când erai mic? Mi-l cânți?*
38. What are you proudest of? — *Cu ce te mândrești cel mai mult?*
39. What would you like your great-grandchildren to know about you? — *Ce ai vrea să știe strănepoții despre tine?*
40. Is there a story nobody has asked you about yet? — *E vreo poveste pe care nu ți-a cerut-o nimeni până acum?*

## After the conversation

Write down the names, places and years you heard while they are fresh, and add them to your family tree with the recording on the person's profile. On Cartea Neamului you can invite cousins and aunts to the same private tree, so the stories reach the whole family and others can add what they remember. A printable version of these questions in Romanian is in our [questions for grandparents tool](/unelte/intrebari-pentru-bunici-pdf).

## Frequently asked questions

### My Romanian is weak. Should I still ask in Romanian?

Yes, read the question in Romanian even if it sounds stiff. Your grandparents will understand, and they will answer in the language of the memory. You can translate the recording later with a relative's help.

### My grandparents do not want to talk about communism. What do I do?

Do not push. Ask about everyday things first: food, school, holidays. The hard stories often come later, when they decide to tell them.

### How do I keep the recordings safe?

Keep a copy outside the phone, attached to the person in the family tree, and share them only with relatives you trust.

### Can relatives in Romania help?

Often they know the village, the nicknames and the cousins. Invite them to the same tree and ask them to add what they know.
